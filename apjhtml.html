Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam

The Missile Man & People's President of India

Early Life

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam was born on 15 October 1931 in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu. He came from a modest family; his father owned a boat and worked as a local imam, while his mother managed the household. To help support the family, young Kalam sold newspapers. Despite financial struggles, his teachers remembered him as a bright, hardworking student with a deep love for mathematics.

Education and Early Career

Kalam studied physics at St. Joseph's College, Tiruchirappalli, before moving to Madras to study aerospace engineering. He once dreamed of becoming a fighter pilot but narrowly missed selection for the Indian Air Force. This setback led him toward a career in aerospace science that would go on to shape India's future.

The Missile Man of India

Kalam spent four decades working with the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO). He played a key role in developing India's satellite launch vehicles and ballistic missiles, including the Agni and Prithvi missiles. This work earned him the title "Missile Man of India." He also played a major role in the Pokhran-II nuclear tests of 1998.

The People's President

In 2002, Kalam was elected the 11th President of India, becoming the first scientist and lifelong bachelor to hold the office. Known for his warmth and simplicity, he became fondly known as the "People's President," using his position to inspire the youth of the nation.

"Dream, dream, dream. Dreams transform into thoughts, and thoughts result in action."

A Life of Simplicity

Even after his presidency, Kalam lived a simple life. He owned little more than a few books, his veena, some clothes, and a laptop. He remained a lifelong teetotaler and vegetarian, and spent much of his later years teaching and interacting with students across India.

Final Days and Legacy

Kalam passed away on 27 July 2015 after collapsing while delivering a lecture at IIM Shillong, doing what he loved most — teaching. He was laid to rest in his hometown of Rameswaram with full state honours. His legacy continues to inspire millions of students across India and the world.

In loving memory of Dr. A. P. J. Abdul Kalam (1931 – 2015)
